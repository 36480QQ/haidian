Regional collaboration through accountable handovers

Consent, authority and verifiable outputs precede collaboration. The Beiwei Community interface adds an early-product test entry; other interfaces support scientific testing, public explanation, remediation and cross-region adaptation.

Beiwei Community → A1/A2/A3

Willing teams supply public product issues and synthetic samples. A1 co-tests rules, A2 tests explanations and staffed referrals, and A3 retests corrections, returning versioned gaps and exit receipts.

Scientific methods → local review

The Future Science City interface supports controlled rule simulation; the Huairou interface explores scientific tools and public explanation. Reviewed outputs return to A1 or A2 respectively.

Industry and regions → reusable methods

The E-Town interface co-tests enterprise services; Jing-Jin-Ji interfaces examine local applicability. The Zhongguancun wing shares methods; the Xiaoyue River wing receives controlled feedback.

These are proposed interfaces, not commitments from districts, institutions or teams. Do not start without accountability, permission, maintenance and resources.

Three pilots: from workflow to acceptance evidence

Freeze inputs and rule versions, retaining failed and insufficient-evidence cases. Rule checks do not assess AI quality or establish that a live pilot has passed.

S03 Multi-agent collaboration

Input: synthetic issues and human responsibility baseline. Inject conflict, missing evidence and absent sign-off. Route conflicts to staff and prevent progression without evidence and sign-off.

S06 Scenario marketplace

Input: synthetic applications and admission checklist. Inject expired permission, withdrawal and missing maintenance ownership. Reject or exit; require new review, with no certification or procurement.

S10 Community health sentinel

Input: synthetic aggregate demand and a professional alert baseline. Inject small samples, noise and unavailable staff. Insufficient evidence cannot pass; pause without staff; no individual diagnosis.

Twelve-week controlled pilot

Six two-week windows produce evidence and apply stop rules. Failed access control, inaccessible ordinary service or unresolved responsibility prevents progression.

Weeks 01–02

Lock baseline

Issue list, data boundary, responsible roles and ordinary service.

Pause without accountable ownership
Weeks 03–04

Desktop replay

Public, synthetic or authorised de-identified data.

Return if results cannot be reproduced
Weeks 05–06

Failure injection

Access breach, error, conflict and stop drills.

Terminate if the system cannot stop
Weeks 07–08

Public walkthrough

Plain language, accessibility and staffed routes.

Pause if equivalent service is inaccessible
Weeks 09–10

Coordinate recovery

Joint review, challenge, correction receipt and recovery.

Retain human workflow if conflict remains
Weeks 11–12

Independent review

Continue, remediate or exit with a public summary.
