# S01 / S06 / S07 Human–AI Symbiotic Scenario Transfer Validation

> Status: conceptual protocol-transfer validation. S01 has passed 24 synthetic rule tests; S06 and S07 have not been field-tested. This file is not implementation or performance evidence.

All three scenarios use the same five required fields—one public task, an AI path, a human/offline path, shared space, and stop/recovery—but they do not copy one spatial solution.

| Scenario | Same task and two paths | Shared space | Stop threshold | Restore gate | Exit assets |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S01 accessible arrival | AI explains a verified route; fixed wayfinding, a paper route and published human-service status complete the same journey | Entry marker, fixed map, status board and receipt point | Any critical accessible segment is blocked or service hours are unverified | Route, accessibility and service-status review plus named-role sign-off | Disable dynamic advice, update status, keep fixed/paper paths and delete the minimal receipt |
| S06 first use | AI explains product version, capability and limits; human and paper explanations allow account-free refusal | Reversible trial table, limits notice, version board and human explanation point | Version conflict or an unexplained capability/data boundary | Version, limitation, privacy-safety and staffing checks all pass | Remove the table and wrong version notice, keep paper explanation and delete temporary input |
| S07 robot observation | AI explains device state and raises alerts; human explanation, physical separation, emergency stop and ordinary bypass remain | Observation line, speed/range notice, human emergency-stop point and exit zone | Boundary breach, loss of contact, unavailable emergency stop or pedestrian/vehicle conflict | Device reset, safety review, emergency-stop test and named attendant confirmation | Remove the robot, reset barriers, keep the bypass and seal the incident record |

Transfer finding: the same protocol changes the station’s first metre, whether a trial table may enter public space, and whether a robot may continue operating. The mechanism transfers; the spatial consequence does not repeat.
