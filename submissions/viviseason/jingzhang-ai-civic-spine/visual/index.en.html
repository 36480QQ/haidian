Four Graphic Languages

Each figure lists only the objects actually shown. Land-use colors do not carry the meaning of a district, corridor, or evidence state.

Civic spine

A deep-red solid line only means the Jing-Zhang heritage and civic-service spine. It communicates a continuous design intent, not a road redline.

Public-life interface

A green band only means an interface for staying, service, and observation. It is adjacent to the mobility spine, not the same object.

Key area

Key areas use a common white field, dark outline, and number. Blue, green, and orange are no longer used as substitutes for district meaning.

Provisional boundary

A grey dashed line only marks the provisional constraint. It is deliberately downplayed so it cannot be mistaken for an official control line.

From Spatial Proposal to Release Review

Spatial proposal, data, and human judgement are not peer objects. They answer in order: what to do, where to do it, and when it may open.

1

Make the spatial move explicit

Figures 01 to 04 identify the relationship among the civic spine, public-life interface, key areas, mobility nodes, and design land uses.

2

Confirm users and staffed fallback

Each AI scenario must state who uses it, who interprets it, and how service continues when an app or automated service fails.

3

Decide whether public trial is allowed

Only when access, public life, data boundaries, and pause conditions are professionally reviewable may a limited, reversible trial begin.

Evidence Boundary

The available OSM mobility and building layers are only low-contrast map context. They do not prove public life, comfort, accessibility compliance, ownership, or statutory road conditions.

Therefore the public-life interface is a design proposition. Before implementation, the network, entrances, crossings, edges, and public-life observations need rights-cleared data or local professional review.
