Three People-First Tests

Jane Jacobs informs the diversity test: innovation depends on mixed primary uses, short and optional paths, buildings of varied ages and costs, active ground-floor edges, and repeated everyday encounters rather than the scale of a single-purpose technology park Source. Peter Newman and Jeffrey Kenworthy inform the low-car test: station access, walking, cycling, and public transport must structure the plan before a smart-mobility interface is added Source. Jan Gehl informs the public-life test: the design must first observe who walks, stays, sits, talks, and uses public services, then decide which spatial components and AI scenarios are justified Source.

These are veto tests, not stylistic references. A public-space project serving only one enterprise does not proceed without diversity of uses and times; a continuous walking and cycling corridor is not claimed without a verified pedestrian network and station-access baseline; and safety, comfort, or vitality is not claimed without anonymized public-life observations. Because the participant cannot visit the site, this revision uses a two-level evidence model. OSM, official public directories, and open remote sensing first establish network, morphology, service, and blue-green proxy baselines, with licenses, queries, raw hashes, coordinate systems, and processing limitations recorded for every item. A local professional team or rights-cleared community partner must later perform anonymized observation. On 2026-08-17, raw OSM mobility and building snapshots were acquired and clipped, while repeated HTTP 429 responses left an empty POI file and green/water data were not downloaded. Consequently, no OSM-derived values are imported into submission layers or metrics in this revision, and land-use mix, public life, and blue-green performance remain unknown. The first level can support site selection and design diagnosis but cannot replace the second. The full contract is recorded in visual/assets/people-first-research.json.

Coordination of urban character, public space, and building controls returns to the professional standards matrix Standard.

Area ratios describe allocation but do not prove that a place works for people. Open network and remote-sensing data first screen 12 future observation locations and identify breaks, barriers, station entrances, service gaps, and potential shade deficits.
