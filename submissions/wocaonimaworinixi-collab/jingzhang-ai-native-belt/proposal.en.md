# Centennial Jing-Zhang · Origin Force AI-Native Innovation Belt

> One-sentence thesis: a century ago the Jing-Zhang Railway solved how people and goods cross mountains; today this corridor must solve how people and intelligent agents share a city.

**Code name: Origin Force.** The name comes from Beijing’s AI Origin Community—"origin" is not just a coordinate but a reproducible launch capability. The proposal upgrades the Jing-Zhang railway corridor from a passive green buffer into an active **urban operating-system interface**: the ground plane is a park for people; the buildings and underground spaces host computing and data infrastructure; the interface runs public services that citizens can read, regulators can audit, and agents can invoke.

## Design Basis and Source List

This is a formal-stage AI-agent submission. The primary source is the *Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt Urban Design International Open Call Prequalification Announcement* and the agent-facing open-call taskbook [source:OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT] [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK]. All spatial judgments rely on the provisionally registered rough boundary, key-area enumeration, value ranges and source inventory in `brief/site-package/`. No unpublished spatial data or third-party protected drawings are used. The generation workflow is fixed in four steps: read the design brief and allowed design space; read the source registry to determine usage boundaries for each item; build a four-way alignment of scope, tasks, depth and metrics; then produce narrative text, geometry, drawings and self-check results.

Source usability is strictly tiered to prevent reference-only materials from being misused as scoring evidence [source:SOURCE-REGISTRY]. Materials are classified as formal-use, background-only or provisional-only; only formal-use materials participate in metric recalculation and compliance arguments. Any judgment that depends on background or provisional data is labeled as an assumption and recorded in `assumptions.json`.

The processed fact pack is a reading navigation layer, not a new authoritative source [source:PROCESSED-FACT-PACK]. It helps agents organize the three-level scope, three key areas, announcement tasks, agent.1–agent.6 requirements, data availability and gaps into a readable proposal; factual claims must still be checked against the registered original materials.

Current-condition diagnosis covers six dimensions: railway-heritage linear accessibility, spatial distribution of innovation actors, commute and transfer bottlenecks, public-space service blind spots, building-stock age and structure, and the siting potential for data and computing facilities [depth:existing_conditions_diagnosis]. The diagnosis concludes that the corridor does not lack industrial density; it lacks an **interface that turns innovation activity into urban experience**—research happens behind closed buildings while the heritage lies behind fences, and the two rarely meet in the same public space.

## Three-Level Scope Framework

The proposal follows the announcement’s three-level nested framework, with each level carrying distinct precision and responsibility [depth:three_level_scope_framework] [standard:PROJECT-OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT]. The first level, the coordinated research area, answers "what role does this corridor play in the city and industrial landscape"; its output is research conclusions, trend judgments and strategic frameworks, not regulatory drawings. The second level, the overall design area, answers "how urban renewal lands on parcels and streets"; its output reaches the urban-design depth of a regulatory detailed plan, including land-use structure, intensity zoning, height and character controls, and road and blue-green frameworks. The third level, the key areas, answers "how is an ordinary day here changed"; its output reaches the urban-design depth of a comprehensive implementation plan, including building-massing relationships, ground and underground linkages, public interfaces and phasing.

The three levels are not simple scale changes but **tightening responsibilities**. The research level may propose trend assumptions without complete data, provided validation paths are stated; the overall level uses only recalculable metrics and registered geometry; the key-area level must map every spatial action to a verifiable renewal-project number. This discipline lets reviewers judge whether the proposal oversteps at any level, and lets future teams replace one level without overturning the whole framework [source:AGENT-TASKBOOK].

## Coordinated Research Area: Industry and Future-City Research

The coordinated research places the corridor within Beijing’s international science and technology innovation center. Its uniqueness lies not in how many AI firms it hosts, but in the triple overlay of **centennial industrial-heritage narrative capital**, a **university-and-institute talent pump**, and a **real-world urban testbed**. Few corridors worldwide combine all three, so the proposal positions the corridor as an upgrade from an industry cluster to a **prototype AI-native city segment** [depth:overall_spatial_structure] [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK].

To keep the position from becoming a slogan, the proposal benchmarks ten transferable mechanisms rather than formal copies:

| Benchmark Type | Reference Experience | Mechanism Transferred to Jing-Zhang |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Railway-heritage linear park | High Line-type linear park | Make continuous walking rights the first delivered infrastructure |
| University–industry interface | Campus-city fusion blocks | Replace walled campuses with open ground-floor interfaces |
| Industrial-heritage innovation conversion | Old-factory innovation clusters | Retain structure, change function, make the conversion process public |
| City-scale testbed | Open-street pilots | Exchange tiered right-of-way for real-scenario data |
| Developer community | Open-source community physicalization | Reserve no-application activity spaces for non-registered actors |
| Science communication facility | Urban science centers | Force research outputs into public exhibit formats |
| Nighttime innovation economy | 24-hour innovation districts | Couple nighttime space use with tidal compute electricity pricing |
| Data governance | Urban data trusts | Return part of data revenue to public-space maintenance |
| Youth housing | Talent co-ownership housing | Trade housing stability for talent retention |
| Urban-agent governance | Algorithmic impact assessment | Maintain public explainability dashboards and veto channels |

Future-city research identifies three variables that must be reserved for now [source:SITE-PACKAGE]. First, **embodied-agent right-of-way**: robot delivery, inspection and low-speed shuttle services will become normal within five to ten years; without reserved low-speed lanes and handoff points, future retrofitting will sacrifice pedestrian space. Second, **urbanized computing**: edge compute nodes will move from data centers to street blocks; their heat, power and noise must be pre-configured in municipal systems. Third, **human–machine public psychology**: citizen acceptance of cameras, sensors and autonomous devices varies with explainability; transparency is therefore a spatial element.

## Overall Design Area: Urban Renewal and Regulatory-Plan-Level Urban Design

The overall spatial structure is summarized as **one belt, three cores, five gates and many touchpoints** [depth:overall_spatial_structure]. The belt is the Origin Force Belt, a continuous linear public space along the railway corridor that is not interrupted by motor vehicles. The three cores are the functional kernels of the three key areas. The five gates are transfer portals between rail stations and neighborhoods, handling passenger distribution, information release and robot handoff. The touchpoints are small, dense, replaceable AI+ scenario nodes.

Urban-design controls split into mandatory and flexible layers [standard:MOHURD-CONTROL-DETAILED-PLANNING]. Mandatory controls include: the belt’s continuity may not be broken by new motor-vehicle roads; a minimum public-open ratio is required for ground floors along the belt; heritage-structure sight corridors are protected; public-space quantity in key areas may not decrease due to development adjustments. Flexible controls include: internal functional mixing ratios may float within set ranges; building heights may seek localized exceptions through design review within district caps; phasing may be adjusted according to implementation actors, but the sequence logic may not change.

Intensity zoning uses three descending levels: medium-high around the cores for industry-service mixing; medium in transition blocks for housing and community services; and low in the heritage core view-shed for public space and cultural facilities [depth:development_intensity_controls]. Height controls correspond, creating a stepped profile from core to belt so that the sky and heritage silhouette remain visible from the belt [depth:height_massing_character].

The renewal principle is "three no’s": no large-scale demolition, no homogeneous facades, no enclosed campuses. Renewal actions are limited to four types—function change while retaining structure, ground-floor interface opening, roof and underground mixed use, and street-section reallocation—each with verifiable criteria [depth:retain_renovate_demolish].

Character uses four color tones: **iron gray** for railway components and compute enclosures; **brick red** for existing industrial and institute buildings; **natural wood** for public-space furniture and slow-mobility facilities; **mist white** for new lightweight structures. The rule is not decoration but honesty: new additions are visually distinguishable from old fabric, preserving the legibility of a century of layering [depth:height_massing_character].

## Detailed Design of Key Areas

The three key areas are fixed by the registered enumeration; the proposal does not add or remove them, but deepens their internal design [source:KEY-AREA-SOURCE] [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design]. Beijing AI Origin Community handles "daily life"; Dazhongsi AI Industry Cluster handles "industry display"; Zhongzhiyuan AI Acceleration Area handles "research incubation". They are linked by the Origin Force Belt into a continuous experience sequence from living to production to creation.

**Beijing AI Origin Community** translates the abstract "origin" concept into accessible public space. The proposal places the Origin Dome, a semi-outdoor community living room, at the community center; it is open co-working during the day and community screenings and lectures at night. Streets use a "slow street" section where motor vehicles are speed-limited and yield to pedestrians, strollers, wheelchairs and low-speed robots. Ground-floor commercial interfaces reserve variable display niches for local teams to show prototypes for free, creating a daily sense that "research happens on the street" [depth:three_key_area_detailed_design].

**Dazhongsi AI Industry Cluster** stages a dialogue between heritage and computing. The existing steam-locomotive water-tower-like structure is converted into the Steam & Tensor Tower, its exterior carrying a low-resolution light array that maps the campus compute load in real time—showing rhythm, not data content. The Pendulum Walk runs toward Dazhongsi, juxtaposing the temporal sense of bells with the sequential sense of data. The cluster interior is organized as courtyard blocks to avoid super-massive volumes that sever pedestrian continuity [depth:height_massing_character].

**Zhongzhiyuan AI Acceleration Area** accelerates without enclosing. The proposal removes continuous solid walls and replaces them with topography, water and planting boundaries; an open experimental facade faces the belt, allowing the public to observe selected experiments through windows. The area includes the Sanchakou Agent Station, a concentrated point for robot battery swap, vehicle handoff and operations duty, gathering dispersed autonomous-device activities into a manageable location [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure].

Eight narrative landmarks are distributed along the belt as memorable spatial anchors: Origin Marker; Steam & Tensor Tower; Switchback Theater; Data Market; Pendulum Walk; Agent Station; Memory Tunnel; and Origin Dome. They share a material and signage language and are all free of charge, ensuring that narrative capital exists as a public good [depth:blue_green_public_space].

## AI Innovation Ecosystem, Personas, and AI+ Scenarios

The innovation-ecosystem goal is not to attract more firms, but to **lower the threshold for turning an idea into an urban experiment**. The proposal builds four nested circles: research actors at the core; scenario and data supply in the next ring; capital and policy services outside that; and public participation and oversight at the outer ring [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK]. Each circle maps to physical space—research inside buildings, scenarios on streets, services at gates, participation in plazas—so the ecosystem does not remain on an organizational chart.

Eight personas drive spatial requirements, each mapped to a verifiable design action [depth:renewal_project_list]:

| Persona | Key Need | Corresponding Spatial Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Model-training engineer | Safe late-night commute and food | Continuous nighttime lighting and 24-hour service points |
| Embodied-intelligence entrepreneur | Continuous real-world test surface | Tiered-right-of-way test loop |
| Data-labeling crowdworker | Flexible micro-employment near home | Community shared workstations linked to childcare |
| AI ethics researcher | Cross-institutional debate venue | Origin Dome and Switchback Theater |
| Railway-heritage volunteer guide | Tangible artifacts and gathering points | Heritage-node interpretation stands and assembly plazas |
| High-school AI club student | Low-threshold hands-on space | Data Market weekend open workshops |
| Robot-delivery operator | Parking, swap and maintenance | Sanchakou Agent Station |
| Parent-commuter product manager | Barrier-free continuous path and childcare | Slow-street section and gate childcare points |

Twenty AI+ scenarios are organized under six registered scenario families, each with a site, responsible actor and kill condition. Mobility and walkability include adaptive signal timing, bottleneck detection, event-day crowd flow, and barrier-free real-time routing. Enterprise services include policy matching, scene marketplace, compute-and-data brokerage, and compliance self-check. Public safety includes nighttime security review, crowd-density warning, equipment-fault linkage, and emergency-drill simulation. Cultural guide includes heritage augmented explanation, multilingual guidance, and historical-image overlay. Health service includes community health navigation and chronic-disease follow-up reminders. Robotics includes low-speed delivery, environmental inspection, and public-facility cleaning. All scenarios obey three red lines: no identifiable personal video used for training, no undisclosed behavior scoring in public space, and every automated decision reserves a human appeal entry [depth:risk_missing_data].

Scenario spatial forms are deliberately small. The proposal opposes giant "AI exhibition centers" and instead substitutes dense small touchpoints—typically no larger than a convenience store, buildable or removable within two weeks. This reversibility lets the city experiment quickly and prevents failed scenarios from leaving expensive vacant buildings [depth:renewal_project_list].

## Land Use, Building Scale, and Retain-Renovate-Demolish Strategy

Land use follows mixing rather than zoning, but mixing has boundaries: every block must contain at least two of the three categories—housing, industry services and public services—and large-area warehousing or logistics are prohibited in the heritage core view-shed [depth:land_use_layout] [standard:MNR-LAND-USE-CLASSIFICATION-GUIDE]. Land-use categories strictly follow the national classification guide to avoid creating non-transferable custom classes.

Building-scale projection is built on a recalculation chain: start from the provisional boundary area, apply the upper intensity limits of each zone, subtract public space and non-buildable areas, and derive a developable-scale range [metric:site_area_sqm] [metric:floor_area_ratio]. Because the boundary is provisional, all area-derived figures are given as ranges and marked for recalculation in `metrics.json`; once official precise geometry is provided, only the base area needs replacement to recalculate automatically [depth:metrics_recalculation].

The retain-renovate-demolish strategy uses four levels with public and verifiable criteria [depth:retain_renovate_demolish]:

- **Retain** (original-state protection): railway-heritage structures with clear historical information receive only structural reinforcement and safety improvement.
- **Renovate** (structure kept, function changed): existing buildings with safe structures and adaptable spatial scale keep their frames while ground floors and building services are upgraded.
- **Infill** (small-scale new construction): small insertions only where block interfaces are broken, used to reconnect fragmented public edges.
- **Demolish** (conditional): strictly limited to buildings with structural safety hazards, severe public-access blockages or fire-code conflicts, and only after a replacement solution exists.

The proportion guideline is clear: retain plus renovate should dominate, and demolition is minimized. The proposal does not provide a building-by-building list because current-building and property censuses are organizer data gaps; instead it provides decision rules and a recalculation template so the list can be generated mechanically once data arrive [depth:risk_missing_data].

## Transport, Rail, Municipal Infrastructure, and Public Services

The first transport principle is **return continuity to walking** [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking]. The Origin Force Belt is continuous for walking and cycling throughout; crossings of urban arterials use underpasses or overpasses rather than at-grade signals. The five gates transfer between rail stations and neighborhoods, each containing four standard components: barrier-free continuous path, concentrated bicycle parking, information interface, and robot handoff bay. A gate is not a building but a recognizable kit of spatial devices that can be flexibly combined at different station conditions.

Road sections are reallocated into four bands: pedestrian, slow-traffic, low-speed and motor. The low-speed band is narrow, used by robot delivery and low-speed shuttles, separated from sidewalks by paving and level change and from motor lanes by hard barriers. This low-speed band is the proposal’s most critical spatial reservation for the embodied-intelligence era: it can serve as a widened non-motor lane today and accommodate autonomous devices in the future without reconstruction [depth:traffic_rail_slow_parking].

Parking uses total-volume control and shared调剂: new surface parking is原则上 not added, existing lots open to community sharing at night, and industry and residential blocks share parking through peak-hour错峰. Event-day traffic organizes temporary pedestrianized sections, shuttle loops and one-way pedestrian flows.

The municipal chapter adds **new infrastructure** beyond conventional utilities [depth:municipal_new_infrastructure]. Edge compute nodes are distributed, small-scale and close to use scenarios; their siting avoids residential main orientations, noise limits are set for cooling equipment, and waste-heat recovery interfaces are reserved. Power is dual-circuit with tidal调节 capacity coupled with nighttime compute pricing. Data transmission uses underground shared trenches to avoid repeated excavation. Sensing devices follow minimum-necessity principles; all locations are shown on a public map with collection type and retention period.

Public services follow the 15-minute life-circle standard, with three additions for the corridor population: youth-oriented community kitchens and shared kitchens; childcare衔接 points at gates and key areas for parent-commuters; and convertible public-service spaces that serve community needs on weekdays and public science workshops on weekends. All additions map to specific renewal-project numbers [depth:renewal_project_list].

## Blue-Green Network, Public Space, and Urban Character

The blue-green system uses the Origin Force Belt as the main vein, with fingers penetrating into neighboring blocks as a "leaf-vein" structure [depth:blue_green_public_space]. The main vein provides continuous passage, tree shade and stormwater detention; fingers use leftover corners, setback spaces and roofs to form a dense network of small green points. The proposal rejects large ornamental lawns in favor of accessible, usable functional greenery, with tree canopy coverage high enough to create continuous summer shade [metric:green_ratio].

Stormwater uses source-distributed control: bioswales and sunken green spaces along the belt, permeable paving in hard plazas, and rainwater reuse facilities in new projects in key areas. Blue elements are mainly small water features and dry streams, avoiding large water surfaces with high maintenance and safety burdens.

Public-space quality is measured not by area but by **use density and accessible hours**. The proposal requires a sit-and-stop node at regular intervals along the belt, each with four basic elements: shade, seating, drinking water and lighting; nighttime lighting creates a continuous recognizable safe path without dark gaps. Public-space quantity in the overall design area has a minimum that may not be reduced by development adjustments [metric:public_space_ratio].

Character continues the four-tone palette and adds three operational rules [depth:height_massing_character]. First, silhouette before facade: the skyline profile seen from the belt is controlled, while individual facades may vary. Second, old and new are distinguishable: additions differ in material and construction from original fabric, without historicist imitation. Third, equipment is landscape: compute cabinets, ventilation shafts and robot stations are not hidden but presented in a unified iron-gray language, becoming part of the corridor’s technological character.

Night lighting is separately controlled. Three brightness levels are defined: heritage nodes with low-level accent lighting; public-activity nodes with medium-level lighting; and general blocks with base lighting. Large media facades and dynamic advertising are prohibited to avoid light pollution affecting residents and heritage atmosphere. The Steam & Tensor Tower light array is limited to low brightness and slow rhythm, serving as environmental background rather than visual focus.

## Renewal Projects, Implementation Policy, and Phasing

The renewal-project list is organized by "minimum implementable unit" so that each project can be initiated and effective independently [depth:renewal_project_list]. Categories are: public-space continuity, interface opening, heritage activation, new infrastructure, and public-service addition. The list does not specify investment amounts because cost benchmarks are an organizer data gap; instead it provides scale parameters and implementation-difficulty ratings for subsequent teams to apply local cost indices [depth:risk_missing_data].

Implementation policy proposes sixteen mechanisms forming an adoptable "mechanism list":

| ID | Mechanism | Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M01 | Compute vouchers | Lower the threshold for small teams to access compute |
| M02 | Scene marketplace | Regularly publish city test scenes open for application |
| M03 | Data trust | Return part of data revenue to public-space maintenance |
| M04 | Tiered right-of-way | Allocate low-speed ROW by device type and time period |
| M05 | Model registry wall | Public registration interface for models used in public scenes |
| M06 | Heritage digital twin | Comparable archive of pre- and post-renovation states |
| M07 | Ethics review board | Independent review for public-facing scenarios |
| M08 | Explainability dashboard | Explain automated decisions in citizen language |
| M09 | Nighttime compute tidal pricing | Guide compute off-peak and couple with nighttime space use |
| M10 | Flexible innovation land term | Shorten land lease terms for experimental uses |
| M11 | Talent co-ownership housing | Trade housing stability for talent retention |
| M12 | City data open interfaces | Provide public data through standard APIs |
| M13 | Regulatory sandbox | Fault-tolerant trials within limited time and space |
| M14 | Public veto channel | Suspend scene deployment when opposition threshold is reached |
| M15 | Algorithmic impact assessment | Assess impact on vulnerable groups before deployment |
| M16 | Withdrawal clause | Preset failure exit and restoration responsibility for every scene |

M14 and M16 are the two most easily overlooked yet most critical. The former gives the public a real brake; the latter forces deployers to budget and assign responsibility for failure before breaking ground. Without these two, so-called smart-city pilots easily become irreversible fait accomplis [standard:PROJECT-AGENT-OPEN-CALL-TASKBOOK].

Phasing follows "connect first, open second, densify third" [depth:phasing_implementation]:

- **Near term**: connect the Origin Force Belt main vein for walking and cycling, build basic gate components, land a small number of reversible AI+ touchpoints, and complete safety improvement and digital-twin archiving of heritage nodes.
- **Mid term**: advance interface opening and function change of existing buildings, build core landmarks and public-service additions, expand tiered-ROW testing, and launch the data trust and compute-voucher mechanisms.
- **Long term**: complete remaining renewal projects, form a complete touchpoint network and ecosystem service system, and adjust the mechanism list and spatial configuration based on mid-term evaluation results.

Assessment thresholds are set between phases: before entering the next phase, public evaluation, scenario survival-rate statistics and metric recalculation must be completed; projects that fail to meet thresholds may be delayed or canceled, and standards may not be lowered to meet schedules. This makes phasing not just a timeline but a feedback-based implementation discipline.

## Metrics, Area Recalculation, and Compliance Matrix

The metric system uses `metrics.json` as the sole authoritative carrier; the narrative only explains, avoiding inconsistency between text and data [depth:metrics_recalculation]. Core metrics include site area, building footprint area, green ratio, public-space ratio, floor area ratio and key-area count [metric:site_area_sqm] [metric:key_area_count]. All area units are square meters and length units are meters, consistent with the repository unit convention.

Area recalculation hinges on **declaring precision rather than manufacturing precision**. The current boundary is provisional, so all area-derived metrics are marked for recalculation, with formulas and dependencies retained in the data files [source:BOUNDARY-SOURCE]. The recalculation interface is designed so that once official precise geometry arrives, replacing the base area automatically recalculates green ratio, public-space ratio and FAR without rewriting the proposal’s spatial conclusions [metric:green_ratio] [metric:building_footprint_area_sqm]. This separation of conclusions from base numbers is the proposal’s most important technical response to data gaps.

The compliance matrix is carried by three files: `compliance_matrix.json` covers announcement task items and the six agent-facing requirements; `standard_matrix.json` covers responses to national and industry standards; and `design_depth_matrix.json` covers fifteen design-depth items [standard:MOHURD-URBAN-DESIGN-MEASURES] [standard:MOHURD-ARCH-DESIGN-DEPTH-2016]. Every entry in the three matrices must point to specific narrative sections, drawings, geometry layers and metric names; entries with only conclusions and no evidence locations are not allowed.

Self-check results are recorded in `self_check.json`, including pass, unknown and not-applicable judgments; any item judged as a blocking failure prevents the proposal from entering review, so self-check is not self-praise but an **active problem-disclosure list**. The proposal’s known blocking items are concentrated in organizer data gaps and are honestly labeled in the file [depth:risk_missing_data].

## Risk, Copyright, and Compliance

Risk identification covers eight dimensions: data privacy, implementation complexity, public acceptance, operations cost, policy uncertainty, spatial dispute, technology maturity, and equity and inclusion. Three are rated as high attention [depth:risk_missing_data]. First, data privacy: public-space sensing devices easily overreach; the proposal responds with minimum-necessity, public mapping of device locations, and stated retention periods, and requires a human appeal entry for any automated decision affecting individuals. Second, public acceptance: visible technical facilities trigger anxiety; the proposal uses explainability dashboards and a public veto channel as institutional buffers. Third, equity and inclusion: AI scenarios tend to favor tech-savvy users; the proposal requires every public-facing scenario to provide a non-digital alternative so older adults and non-smart-device users are not excluded.

Implementation risks center on fragmented property rights and coordination costs. The response is to cut renewal projects into minimum implementable units so that stalling in any unit does not block overall progress; phasing assessment thresholds prevent forced large-scale retrofitting under immature conditions. Operations-cost risk is partly hedged by the data trust and shared-open mechanisms, but the proposal does not promise specific revenue levels.

Copyright and source compliance: all drawings, geometry, metrics and text are generated by the participating agent based on publicly registered materials in the repository, without using any unpublished spatial data or third-party protected drawings [source:SOURCE-REGISTRY]. Cited standards and regulations are referenced only for their clause requirements, not copied in full. Copyright details are in `report/copyright_statement.md`; license is community-display only.

AI participation is disclosed truthfully: the proposal was produced by an AI agent that performed material reading, structural organization, scheme generation, geometry construction, drawing layout and self-check execution; agent identity and generation method are recorded in `agent.json`. The proposal acknowledges three limitations: no site visit, so spatial experience judgments are inferred from materials; no official precise geometry, so area conclusions are ranges; and no current-building or property census, so retain-renovate-demolish provides rules rather than a building-by-building list. The proposal does not claim approval or endorsement by any authority and makes no promises about implementation outcomes; all conclusions must be reviewed by qualified professionals before use in statutory procedures [standard:PROJECT-OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT].
