# Jing-Zhang Intelligence Commons: Spatial-First Urban Design V4

This English companion summarizes the Chinese formal proposal. The design treats the Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt as civic infrastructure rather than a closed technology park or a set of screens. The primary move is a public urban structure: one heritage and slow-mobility spine, three civic courts, two innovation wings, and twelve AI+ scenarios attached to real spatial nodes.

The main change in V4 is representational discipline. The boards now start with a dominant overall urban design plan. Metrics, evidence marks, governance cards, and algorithmic rules are kept at the edge of the drawing or overlaid onto specific spatial objects. A reviewer should first understand the city structure from a distance, then inspect the computation, data provenance, public-value passport, and stop conditions at close range.

The three key areas are differentiated. The northern Zhongzhiyuan area becomes a low-density testing courtyard for model evaluation, red-team testing, edge intelligence, and robot slow-mobility pilots. The central Beijing AI Origin Community becomes an oblique open-source station, connecting universities, communities, parks, and transit through a shared ground floor. The southern Dazhongsi district becomes a station-city double hall for international demos, civic exhibitions, and compliant data literacy. Each area is represented through a plan, a section, ground-floor public-interface rules, and an operational sequence from evidence lock to reversible retention or removal.

The computational idea is not a separate flow chart. It is a spatial contract. Public access, data minimization, non-digital alternatives, human review, evidence levels, RACI ownership, service levels, stop thresholds, rollback actions, and retirement assets are attached to lines, polygons, nodes, and phases. The algorithm exposes options and recalculation paths; it does not automatically decide demolition, floor area ratio, traffic operations, or public-resource allocation.

The submission remains explicit about evidence limits. The site boundary and key areas are provisional. Building scale, FAR, height, density, setbacks, ownership, utilities, structural condition, fire safety, heritage status, and implementation funding remain unknown until official datasets and professional surveys are available. Known metrics are recalculated from the package GeoJSON; unknown metrics are deliberately not invented.

Implementation is treated as a portfolio rather than a slogan. Eight projects are organized by lead-owner type, partners, prerequisites, CAPEX/OPEX magnitude bands, KPI/SLO, and stop conditions. JZ-01 and JZ-06 are public-space and mobility works that require road-line, access, right-of-way, flow, and accessibility audits. JZ-02, JZ-03, and JZ-05 are reversible prototype and operating-platform projects that require ownership, structure, fire-safety, data-classification contracts, and risk registers. JZ-04 is a long-term station-city project and remains blocked until statutory transport, capacity, ownership, and approval paths are confirmed.

Reference cases are translated as mechanisms rather than copied as styles. AA Landscape Urbanism informs multi-scale mapping, GIS, scripting, and policy-spatial coupling. Foster + Partners examples inform infrastructure-led public realm, adaptive phasing, shaded walkability, and industrial waterfront regeneration. Arup informs integrated mobility, water, energy, data, and implementation systems. These references are not endorsements, and no external imagery or proprietary visual language is reused.

Public-source boundaries are explicit. The official open-call announcement, the agent taskbook, provisional repository geometry, and professional standards are used as the primary review basis. International cases, peer pull requests, and web references are used only for mechanism comparison and delivery-depth calibration; they do not substitute for local statutory planning controls, official geometry, or implementation approval.
