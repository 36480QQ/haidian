# Jingzhang New Era · Creative AI Time-Track — Formal Submission for the Centennial Jing-Zhang AI Innovation Belt Urban Design Open Call

All spatial suggestions are open, co-created reference schemes and do not constitute an approved planning conclusion, government approval, implementation commitment, or engineering feasibility conclusion. > **Core design hypothesis (the first judgment of this proposal).** The Jingzhang New Era · Creative AI Time-Track is a city-scale testbed that, within the reviewed sample (search criteria in §3.2), has no directly comparable case — running autonomous vehicles and robots on the retained original rails along a real railway-heritage park; more fundamentally, it treats **time as the primary planning variable** — a 'Time-Track' framework (timeline × tracks) that lets industry, population, economy, and experience evolve over time instead of freezing into a static blueprint. This is the methodological separation from most static spatial masterplans; the conclusion is a limited-sample observation and not exhaustive; co-track operation still requires heritage and site protection, safety assessment, track ownership, and on-site permit verification [depth:overall_spatial_structure].

### The Time-Track framework: timeline × tracks

The proposal borrows the timeline × track architecture of video editing and elevates it into a development blueprint, replacing the conventional "static spatial masterplan" method [depth:overall_spatial_structure].

**Horizontal = Time — the timeline's forward progress (narrative order).** The timeline threads three spatio-temporal stages from a century of railway history, through present AI empowerment, to boundless future possibility: **historical accumulation → present momentum → future imagination**. It gives the project direction, continuous vitality, and unstoppable forward momentum.

**Vertical = Track — the tracks' vertical stacking (space and dimensions).** In editing, video, audio, effects, and subtitles run in parallel on different tracks; here "track" metaphorizes the project's multi-dimensional industry layout — three tracks laid in parallel, non-interfering, yet overlapping and compounding vertically into a high-density, three-dimensional creative ecosystem:

| Track | Content | Corresponding space/mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Track 3: AI technology / infrastructure base | co-track test line, shared compute, digital twin, permit sandbox | Zhongzhiyuan acceleration area, test-line spine |
| Track 2: cultural heritage / green-ecological industry | century narrative, blue-green ecology, cultural tourism | Jing-Zhang heritage park belt, three landmarks |
| Track 1: city collaboration / developer ecosystem | developer community, employment transition, AI-inclusive services (investment in people) | Origin Community, corridor neighborhoods |

**The final convergence — panoramic intelligent rendering (output).** The horizontal time flow and the vertical track flow, under AI (agent ecosystem / large-model engine), are no longer isolated lines but undergo high-frequency real-time computation and fusion; like the final "mix and pixel render" of audio-video tracks, AI fuses the cross-era, cross-domain "time" and "track" into a single high-fidelity, high-synchronization "Jingzhang epic cut" delivered to the world.

### Four-dimensional dynamics: time as the primary planning variable

![Time-sharing: day and night](assets/figures/04_time_sharing_day_night.jpg)

![Zhongzhiyuan AI accelerator + shared lab](assets/figures/11_zhongzhiyuan_shared_lab.jpg)

The source of separation is a methodological switch: most proposals draw space; this proposal composes space-time — industry, population, economy, and experience all evolve over time rather than freezing into one page.

- **Industry × time = the innovation diffusion clock.** Industry is not a static allocation ("21% research land") but grows along an S-curve: validation (0–2 years, the test line attracts testing demand) → agglomeration (2–5 years, hidden champions set up R&D) → anchoring (5–10 years, anchor headquarters + ecosystem closure). Land-use share is a function of time L(x, t).
- **Population × time = tidal rhythm.** Vitality is not a constant but a time function with amplitude: weekday developer tides, morning/evening commute tides, weekend visitor tides. Tidal amplitude becomes a new human-centric vitality indicator.
- **Economy × time = time-mismatch value.** Compute billed by the minute, testing billed per run (unlike conventional annual rents); renewal revenue recycles into public space via time-mismatched financing. The same space has different value at different times.
- **Experience × time = the time-sharing script.** One day of the co-track test line carries different functions: pre-dawn data training / daytime semi-open testing + commuting / night closed testing / weekend public opening. The same rail, time switches the function.

These four dynamics unify into the operating mechanism of the Time-Track, detailed in the Metrics section (a new time-indicator group: time-sharing ratio, growth rate, tidal amplitude, innovation diffusion stage).

**The four-dimension coupling loop.** The four "× time" terms are not parallel labels but a mutually-driving operating loop: the innovation diffusion clock's industrial agglomeration (the S-curve of L(x,t)) → pulls in developer and visitor flows of the tidal rhythm → those flows misalign across day/night and weekday/weekend, producing minute-billing and per-run-charging time-mismatch value → the time-sharing script distributes that value across 24 hours → recycled revenue feeds back into the co-track test line's iteration, accelerating the next round of agglomeration. Time is thereby upgraded from a background parameter of planning into the first planning variable — the methodological core that separates this proposal from static spatial blueprints, and the shared operational underpinning of the "timeline × tracks" brand narrative and the "physical-track + institutional-track" dual structure: original concept, operating mechanism, and brand narrative close into one loop here.

**POI behavioral-space analysis (existing-data support for L2).** Using 1,127 Amap POI features across 8 layers (966 unique), the corridor's behavioral space — "why people come and what they do" — is initially characterized: education/research (173: 70 universities + 75 research institutes + 28 research academies) combined with 74 science parks and 75 office towers in the land-use layer forms an "industry–academia–office" behavioral hinterland; medical/health (121: 58 community service centers + 33 pharmacies + 30 hospitals) and public space (119: 75 sports venues + 21 libraries) form the daily-service skeleton of the 15-minute life circle; transport supplement (98: 6 metro stations + 50 parking lots + 39 charging stations) shows "rail interchange + parking + charging" travel behavior; cultural heritage (7 relics) forms the linear-heritage behavioral anchor. ### Permit-sandbox rule architecture (concept-level)

> The "machine-verifiable permit sandbox" is specified as a reviewable rule architecture. **Code verification and automated audit are supporting tools; they do not replace statutory licensing, administrative approval, or professional judgment.**

1. **Admission**: legal entities with qualifications, safety plans, test plans, and liability insurance may apply; individuals and entities without responsible ownership are excluded.
2. **Test tiers**: closed (internal) → semi-open (time/area-limited with safety officers) → open (daily) — progressive release by assessment.
3. **Logs and audit**: full telemetry logs retained, auditable, reviewable; key events (collision, hard stop, boundary breach) mandatory reporting.
4. **Human discretion**: safety officers/supervisors hold final discretion over operations; AI is advisory only; no automated decision replaces human judgment on safety and compliance.
5. **Incident halt**: automatic halt on safety events plus human review; testing does not resume until review passes.
6. **Public appeals**: open complaint channel, time-bound response, test suspension possible; suspension/hearing/negotiation triggers on safety and environmental-impact matters (see 10.6).
7. **Exit**: period-end assessment; exit on failure, major incident, or public suspension/reassessment trigger; test data returned or deleted per rules.

### Data and privacy tiers (concept-level)

> Data across S1–S11 is managed in three tiers. **Health, location, and urban-governance data are separately tiered**; "no biometrics collection" is not a blanket privacy answer.

- **Tier 1 (ordinary)**: environmental telemetry, statistical crowd counts, compute usage — minimize, aggregate/desensitize, public by default.
- **Tier 2 (location/behavior)**: trajectories, dwell time, reservations — separate consent, shortest retention, access logging.
- **Tier 3 (health/governance)**: health self-reports and vitals, governance API data — separate management, explicit legal basis (consent/statutory duty), strict audit, independent deletion/exit channels.

Per-scenario notes: S1 no pedestrian biometrics (crowd counts only); S7 health data Tier 3 (explicit consent + medical professional review + deletable); S9 environment only (Tier 1, no personal data); S10 location Tier 2 (shortest retention + can disable); S11 plate Tier 2 (scheduling-minimized, deleted after use); S3/S4 governance data Tier 3 (authorized interface + manual release + no personal data out of sandbox). All scenarios include human review/takeover, appeal, deletion, and exit channels, registered in the asset rights & privacy registry of `sources.json`.

3. **Non-digital booking & payment**: manual windows, phone, and on-site queues coexist; smart booking is not the only entry (S5/S6/S11).
4. **On-site human service**: human desks and assistance retained at key nodes (S6; S8 keeps on-site staff).
5. **Guardian/proxy assistance**: minors, seniors, and persons with disabilities may be assisted by guardians or proxies (S5/S7/S11).
6. **Low-digital-literacy support**: digital-literacy classes and one-on-one assistance; no requirement to master smart devices first (beyond S5 night school).
7. **Emergency help**: one-touch help and emergency response at public spaces and scenario nodes (S1/S8/S9).

The baseline is a concept-level design requirement; implementation follows barrier-free regulations and professional accessibility review, with testing involving seniors and persons with disabilities.

### Public-participation mechanism (concept process)

To avoid unfulfillable blanket terms, participation is specified as a reviewable process:

- **Participants**: corridor residents, merchants, developers, university students, visitors, and minority-group representatives (dedicated channels for seniors/persons with disabilities/low-digital-literacy groups).
- **Veto scope**: for **major safety and environmental-impact matters** (e.g., co-track segment opening, green-space redevelopment, noise-source placement), the proposal recommends setting trigger conditions for suspension, hearing, negotiation, or reassessment; the specific authority, scope, and procedure must be determined by the legally competent body (government and statutory regulators) — this proposal provides conceptual suggestions only and makes no quasi-administrative commitment.
- **Information disclosure**: plans, test schedules, data uses, and monitoring results are published; period and form vary by matter tier.
- **Opinion recording**: a unified opinion register (online + offline), each item numbered and traceable.
- **Dispute handling**: tiered response — general opinions within 7 working days; major objections trigger hearings or negotiation.
- **Feedback-time principle**: time limits are publicly committed; on timeout, the matter escalates to review, public explanation, or an extended deadline — silence is not treated as default consent or adoption.
- **Minority representation**: participation samples cover seniors, persons with disabilities, and low-digital-literacy groups to avoid digital-divide distortion.

**Source legality.** The proposal mainly uses official public documents, public reports, and cleared local data; some policy documents and industry information are pending-verification indicative references (authority level and verification status per entry in `sources.json`, wording aligned accordingly); non-public materials are excluded [source:OFFICIAL-ANNOUNCEMENT].

**Privacy.** Personal data in scenario operations must be de-identified; the test line does not collect pedestrian biometrics.

**Professional review.** Rail-heritage protection, safety assessment, regulatory conditions, municipal capacity, and engineering feasibility require professional review; this proposal provides conceptual direction only.
